## 1. Commerce and retail POS: what each assistant can do, read-only or with actions, and how it is shown

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the two leaders have moved the assistant from a side panel to the front door: Square made its agent (Squaremates) "the main way" US sellers use the Dashboard after sign-in, and Shopify's Sidekick runs multi-step tasks, builds automations and generates custom admin apps, with a proactive task list on the home screen. Both keep a human approval step before any change. Everyone else is one step behind: Lightspeed's acting agent is an opt-in beta, Clover's AI is website and discovery tooling, Epos Now has a support-first assistant with a few catalogue actions, and no merchant-facing assistant was found for SumUp.

### Inferences
- The interaction model at the top of the market in late 2026 has three parts, all present at Square and Shopify: (a) a conversational agent that can act, (b) a proactive list of things that need attention on the home screen (Shopify's task list / Pulse; Square's "flag items needing attention"), and (c) approval before a change is written. No vendor found lets the agent change business data without a human approving.
- "Generated UI" in shipped commerce products means **generated apps and reports**, not forms generated per interaction: Shopify's custom admin apps (36,000 in one quarter), Square's Squaremates Apps, one-sentence ShopifyQL reports with charts. No source showed a mainstream product replacing its fixed admin forms with dynamically generated ones.
- Read-only question answering was the 2025 baseline (Square AI, Lightspeed AI at launch); 2026 is the year acting agents shipped, GA at Square (US) and Shopify, beta at Lightspeed.
- The UK is behind the US on acting agents: Squaremates is US only; UK Square sellers have the earlier Square AI; Lightspeed's agent is a select beta; Clover's tools are US only. A UK product with an acting assistant across the whole back office is ahead of what UK SMB merchants can buy from these vendors today.
- Shopify's tenure data suggests an assistant's job changes over a customer's life: setup and design at the start, analysis later. That supports building both setup tools and reporting tools into one assistant.

## 2. SMB ERP and finance: what has shipped and the interaction model (chat, embedded suggestions, agents with review queues)

### Takeaway
SMB ERP and accounting vendors have converged on named, role-specific agents (payables, sales orders, reconciliation, close) that draft work and stop for human approval, plus a chat assistant and, in 2026, a "workspace" or task pane where agent work is reviewed. Microsoft, Xero, Intuit and Sage have agents in production; NetSuite's new AI-first interface is rolling out by region; SAP's small-business product was not visible in the Joule announcements; Odoo and Zoho expose agent builders and MCP.

## 5. Data-model differences between restaurant and retail, and what unified platforms do

### Takeaway
Retail centres on a stock-keeping unit (a variant with its own code, barcode, price and stock per location); restaurants centre on a prepared order (an item plus modifiers, courses and combos, with stock depleted through ingredients). Square's model, one catalogue where an item has variations and a product type covering goods and services, is the clearest unified pattern found. For a restaurant platform the additions are: item type, SKU and barcode on items and variants, stock on hand per location, returns, and services with duration.

### Cited Findings
- Core difference: restaurant systems organise transactions around prepared orders and service; retail around merchandise, SKUs and inventory. Item structure: menu items, modifiers, courses and combos against SKUs, variants (size, colour) and barcodes. Inventory: retail counts stock per SKU; restaurants deplete ingredients. A hybrid item such as a rotisserie chicken draws down both raw ingredients and a finished-goods count. — [Toast, restaurant vs retail POS](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-vs-retail-pos)
- Hybrids (grocerants, cafés with shelves, markets) need both; two systems create inventory blind spots and inconsistent reports. A restaurant-only POS typically struggles with PLU codes, scale integration and retail inventory; a grocery-only POS lacks kitchen display and modifier depth. If prepared food drives revenue start from the restaurant system and add retail, and the reverse. — [Toast, grocerant POS](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/grocerant-pos)
- Square: one catalogue object for goods and services ("a product for sale or a service for hire"), with a `product_type` on the item (REGULAR, APPOINTMENTS_SERVICE; FOOD_AND_BEV is legacy). Prices and SKUs live on variations (up to 250 per item); a service's variation carries its duration and who can perform it; modifiers are separate lists attached to items; item options define variation axes. — [Square, design a catalog](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/catalog-api/design-a-catalog); [Square, CatalogItemProductType](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/enums/CatalogItemProductType); [Square, CatalogItemVariation](https://developer.squareup.com/reference/square/objects/CatalogItemVariation) (links as recorded in the internal design note `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/CATALOGUE.md`; not re-read this session)
- In sales data Square and Loyverse both sell a *variation* and add modifiers to the line; Loyverse items have variants on up to three option axes with a SKU and a per-store price, plus modifiers with options; Oracle Simphony instead sells menu items with condiment lines and Add / No prefixes. — internal design note `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/SALES_IMPORT.md` (section 2; it marks some vendor field names as from secondary sources)
- The retail industry reference model (OMG ARTS) treats items and services sold as one entity with scan codes attached to the item. Commerce platforms advise keeping the item as the base unit, with shallow category trees and attributes inherited from the category. — [OMG ARTS, items and services sold](https://www.omg.org/retail-depository/arts-odm-73/product_items_and_services_sol.htm); [commercetools, category best practices](https://docs.commercetools.com/learning-model-your-product-catalog/categorization/best-practices-and-advanced-category-management) (as recorded in `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/CATALOGUE.md`)
- Customisation differs by type: food and drink use variations and modifiers heavily; services use variations (length, level) and rarely modifiers; retail goods use variations for size and colour, each with its own SKU and barcode. UK allergen law (the 14 allergens) applies to food and drink only, not to goods or services; VAT follows the thing sold. — `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/CATALOGUE.md` (section 2), citing [Food Standards Agency](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-foods-best-practice-introduction)
- Baseline of the client platform, from its own notes: menu items have one price and no SKU, PLU or barcode; options are a group library with per-dish prices; tax inherits menu, section, item; purchasing has suppliers, quotes, order lists with pars, purchase orders and receiving, but "there is no stock on hand" (pars are a usual quantity). — `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/CATALOGUE.md` (section 1); `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/claude/OUTSTANDING.md` (line 136)
- The internal recommendation already drafted (parked 22 Nov 2026): an `item_type` of food, goods or service inherited menu, section, item like tax; `sku` and `barcode` (GTIN / EAN) on items and on per-item option rows so each size has its own code; the type switches allergens, kitchen routing, stock and duration. Stated as not covered yet: stock counts and reorder points per variant, services as bookings, gift cards (a liability, not a sale), prepacked food labelling. — `/home/user/macaroonie/docs/CATALOGUE.md` (sections 4 and 5)

### Inferences
- Minimum retail additions to a restaurant data model, in dependency order: (1) item type per item; (2) SKU and GTIN barcode on item and variant, with scan-to-sell on the till; (3) stock on hand per variant per location, with movements (sale, receipt, adjustment, transfer, count) so "order to par" can subtract the shelf; (4) returns and exchanges as first-class sales events that put stock back, with a reason and optional restock; (5) purchase orders that receive into stock at cost (a weighted or last cost per variant for margin reports); (6) services with duration and a staff resource, bookable on the existing bookings timeline; (7) tax by item type (already inheritable).
- Second-tier retail features that depend on the first tier: stocktakes, inter-location transfers, supplier catalogues keyed by GTIN, price labels and shelf-edge printing, gift cards and store credit, layaway / customer orders, serial numbers (electronics, warranties) and batch or expiry tracking (food retail, cosmetics). Serial and batch tracking are only needed for some verticals and can be deferred.
- The modifier model does not need to be replaced: a retail variant is an option axis whose rows carry their own code and stock. That is the same choice Square makes (variations for what is stocked, modifiers for how it is prepared).
- The AI consequence: an assistant can only suggest reorders, forecast demand or answer "what is selling through" for goods once stock on hand and cost exist per variant. Stock is the gating data for retail AI, as recipes are for restaurant AI.

### Gaps
- The searches on Square, Loyverse, Shopify POS, Clover and Odoo catalogue documentation did not run, so how Shopify POS, Clover and Odoo model variants, stock by location, serial / batch and restaurant features is not sourced here.
- No vendor-neutral technical source was found on a unified item-to-ingredient-to-stock schema; the Toast material is product marketing.
- **Background (unverified):** Shopify models product, variants with a barcode field, and inventory levels per location, and its POS has no native kitchen or table features; Loyverse also has composite items and stock per store; Odoo uses product templates with attribute-generated variants, lots and serial numbers, multiple warehouses, and a restaurant mode on the same POS; Lightspeed keeps retail and restaurant as separate products; Clover uses item groups with attributes for variants and modifier groups for preparation.

## 7. Direction of travel: agents acting for the merchant, agent-to-agent commerce, and protocols for outside assistants

### Takeaway
Three moves are under way at once: the vendor's own agent becomes the main interface (Square), outside assistants are let in through MCP under the system's permissions (Square, Microsoft, NetSuite, Zoho, Xero), and shoppers' AI assistants are becoming a sales channel through competing protocols (Google-led UCP, OpenAI and Stripe's ACP, AP2 for payment authority). ### Inferences
- For a back-office platform the near-term protocol that matters is MCP for outside assistants (already offered by Square, Microsoft, NetSuite, Zoho), governed by the platform's own roles and permissions and a defined action set. A platform that already has a staff connector with sign-in and confirm cards is aligned with where the incumbents are going.
- Agent-to-agent buying (a merchant's agent ordering from a supplier's system) is at the "fill the supplier's web basket for a human to finish" stage (Square beta), not autonomous purchasing. Supplier ordering with human approval is the realistic 2027 target.
- For shopper-facing commerce a small platform should plan to publish catalogue, availability and ordering through whichever feed the big assistants read, and not build to one protocol while UCP and ACP are both live and checkout behaviour is still changing.
- "Skills" as a distribution unit (NetSuite SuiteSkills, Shopify's saved Skills) suggests vendors expect customers to package repeatable prompts and procedures; this is a lighter alternative to generated forms for tailoring the product to a business.
