## 1. Which vendors have AI assistants or agents for operators, and exactly what can each do?

### Takeaway
By November 2026 almost every large POS and back-office vendor has shipped a conversational assistant over the operator's own data, and the two leaders (Toast and Square) have moved to named agents that draft or carry out work: Toast with scheduled and task agents (Toast IQ Grow and Team, mostly beta, October 2026) and Square with Squaremates (GA in the US, 12 November 2026), which Square calls "the front door to Square Dashboard". UK-rooted vendors are behind on agents: Fourth (iQ 3.0), Access (Evo Copilot), Nory, Tenzo (an MCP connector), Deliverect (menu agents) and Flipdish (phone agent) have shipped, while no native AI assistant was found for Zonal, Tevalis, Vita Mojo, Storekit or Loyverse.

## 2. Read-only question answering or actions? What confirmation or approval step exists?

### Takeaway
Most assistants are still read-only analysts (Lightspeed AI, PAR Coach AI, SpotOn Sam, Tenzo, Access Evo Copilot, NCR Voyix Insight, Crunchtime's AI Analyst, Fourth's recommendations). The vendors that take actions (Toast, Square, SpotOn's menu assistant, Deliverect, Owner, Flipdish) all describe a human approval step for back-office changes, with Square's "propose, approve, execute, undo by asking" the most fully described; the more autonomous agents are confined to low-risk, reversible, guest-facing work (menu ordering and images, review replies, phone answering).

### Cited Findings
- **Toast**: "Toast IQ can make recommendations and, with your approval, take actions across Toast, including in the POS" — [Toast IQ product page FAQ](https://pos.toasttab.com/products/toast-iq)
- Toast actions follow existing permissions: "The data and actions available to individual users in Toast IQ are based on their Toast Web permissions"; some users at a location can edit menus and others cannot — [Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-IQ-Intelligence-Ecosystem)
- Toast menu edits are staged: change names, descriptions and prices, "then publish when ready" — [Toast blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/toast-iq)
- Toast's scheduled agents are notify-only as documented: they check data and send a push notification or email (missed breaks, 86'd items) — [Toast support: Agents Overview](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-IQ-Agents-Overview)
- The Toast Scheduling Agent builds (drafts) schedules from availability, expected sales and overtime risk; the Menu Agent "identifies and executes" menu optimisations — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)
- **Square Managerbot**: "proposes an action, waits for the seller's approval and then executes" — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/new-square-ai-agent-automates-daily-tasks-for-main-street-sellers); it "handles routine workflows with your review and approval", and "you can undo any action Managerbot takes on your account by simply asking Managerbot to reverse the action" — [Square help centre](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8617)

- **Owner.com**: agents "manage and improve each part automatically" (website, ordering, marketing, reviews) — [Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/386548/owner-raises-240m-led-by-goldman-sachs-alternatives-to-build-the-ai-native-platform-for-every-local-business)
- **Voice agents** (SevenRooms, Flipdish) act on bookings and orders directly and escalate complex requests to staff — [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/sevenrooms); [Flipdish](https://www.flipdish.com/gb/resources/blog/introducing-flipdish-ai-phone-agent-for-restaurants)

### Inferences
- A three-tier pattern is visible: (1) ask and answer, universal; (2) propose then approve for changes to operational records (schedules, stock, prices, shifts), which is where Toast and Square are; (3) goal-set autonomy without per-action approval, used only where the change is reversible and guest-facing (menu ranking, images, review replies, campaigns, phone).
- Square's "undo by asking" plus approval is the closest public analogue to confirm cards. Nobody found describes a richer approval surface (diff previews, batched approvals, per-role limits), though that may simply be undocumented.
- Permissions are inherited from the existing role model (Toast), not given to the agent separately. That matches the client's module-permission design.
- Scoping the agent to a defined action list (Square MCP) and to non-substantive fields (Deliverect) are the two guardrail styles vendors advertise.

### Gaps
- No source describes what an approval looks like on screen for Toast (card, diff, button), or which Squaremates action types need approval and which do not.
- Whether any vendor lets an agent place a supplier order unattended was not established. Squaremates "coordinates with suppliers" with seller approval; nothing more specific was found.
- No evidence was gathered on audit trails for agent actions, spend limits, or two-person approval.
- No source found on agents changing prices autonomously in the back office (as opposed to recommending).

- The vendors' own case for integrated suites ("built on the full platform", "one data layer", "context") is the same argument the client's "platform-defined data framework, like a smart ERP" makes: an agent is only as useful as the breadth and consistency of data and actions under it. Single-module vendors (Tenzo) answer by exporting data to someone else's assistant instead.
- 2027 will likely be the year the US leaders' agents go GA and reach enterprise and franchise tiers (Toast agents leaving beta, Squaremates' phased enterprise release, Oracle's command centre). A UK entrant has roughly that long before these become the reference point for buyers, if and when they are offered in the UK.
- Two pricing models are forming: AI free inside the platform to raise attachment (Square, SpotOn) and AI as paid product lines with a named return (Toast). A new platform can choose; the paid lines all attach to revenue or labour cost.
- MCP and CLI access signals that vendors expect operators and their advisers to run general-purpose assistants against the back office. The built-in assistant must be better because of context, confirmation and interface, not because it is the only door.
- Nobody found is promising a generated-interface back office. The agentic roadmap is about work being done and surfaced for approval, with the conventional screens still underneath.
