# Lead with Pepper, keep every screen

Macaroonie should build an AI-first back office in one specific sense: Pepper is the first place to ask and the day arrives as a short list of things to do, while every change still lands in a real, stable screen that a person checks and saves. That is where the market leaders went in 2026 (Square made its agent "the front door" to its dashboard on 12 November, Shopify put a task list on the merchant's home screen, and Microsoft, NetSuite and Sage added surfaces where agents draft and people approve), and no vendor found in restaurant, retail or ERP software has replaced its fixed forms with forms generated on the fly. The three parts of the owner's vision therefore get three different verdicts: natural-language input and navigation, yes, as an addition to the menu and with page jumps that never call the model; dynamically generated forms, no as stated, and yes as real forms that arrive pre-filled plus plain set-up forms drawn the same way every time from the server's own schemas; a platform-set data framework, yes, and it is the most valuable part, built as one registry over what already exists and not as a new metadata engine. Scrutiny tempers the upside: assistants are tried by over half of one vendor's sites and used weekly by about a quarter, chat lowers effort more than it saves time, agents complete roughly a third of open multi-turn business tasks, prompt injection cannot be fully prevented, and at UK price points a mid-tier model would take a third or more of a site's subscription if every click became a model call. Macaroonie starts ahead on the hard half (159 typed tools behind Confirm cards, a 136-subject report catalogue that already works as a semantic layer, a rule-based Today feed, ten form cards, voice and a phone app) and behind on the half that makes it usable and provable: main pages that cannot be opened at a venue and day, three home pages, no review queue, no undo, no usage log, and no test of Pepper against the live model. The recommended order is to measure first, then deep links and one home with an ask bar, then filled forms, a declared action registry with undo, a review queue and the phone, with custom fields and retail last. Confidence in the direction is medium to high; confidence in individual external figures is low, because almost no source page could be opened (findings rest on search-result summaries), several planned searches never ran, and nothing has been tested with Macaroonie users, since there are none yet.

## Agents became the front door in 2026, and every one waits for approval

### Square made its agent the first screen on 12 November 2026

By November 2026 nearly every large till and back-office vendor ships a conversational assistant over the operator's own data, and the two leaders have moved past it. Toast launched its Toast IQ assistant in October 2025 and by October 2026 was selling two families of named agents, Grow for marketing and the new Team for labour, most of them still in beta or early access ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). Square went from a question-answering assistant to Managerbot (open beta, 28 April 2026) to **Squaremates, generally available to US sellers on 12 November 2026**, which it describes as "the front door to Square Dashboard; it's where they ask questions, see what needs attention, and get work done" ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/introducing-squaremates)). Shopify's Sidekick now carries out multi-step tasks, builds automations and generates custom admin apps, with a personalised task list on the merchant's home screen ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-merchant)); merchants held **nearly 34 million conversations with it and built more than 36,000 custom apps in the second quarter of 2026** ([Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/retail-technology/ai-ar-vr/shopify-credits-ai-for-34-revenue-growth-in-q2-2026)). The direction is the same everywhere: from a chat panel beside the product to an agent on the first screen plus a feed of things that need attention.

None of these agents writes business data on its own say-so. Toast IQ acts "with your approval" and within the user's existing permissions ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/products/toast-iq); [Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-IQ-Intelligence-Ecosystem)). Square's agent "proposes an action, waits for the seller's approval and then executes", and an action can be undone by asking the agent to reverse it ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/new-square-ai-agent-automates-daily-tasks-for-main-street-sellers); [Square help centre](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8617)). Shopify states that Sidekick "is never allowed to make changes to your shop without your approval" ([Shopify Help Center](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/productivity-tools/sidekick/features?term=SEO)), and Lightspeed pitches its October 2026 retail agent, still an opt-in beta, on the retailer approving every change ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/news/lightspeed-ai-expands-from-insights-to-action-with-new-agentic-capabilities-for-retail/)). Autonomy without per-change approval appears only where the change is reversible and guest-facing: Deliverect's menu agents re-rank digital menus toward a goal the operator sets, and its assistants "never modify the actual food items themselves" ([The Caterer](https://www.thecaterer.com/indepth/deliverect-launches-ai-agents-and-smart-assistants-to-supercharge-restaurant-sales)).

### The new interface element is a review surface, not a bigger chat

Accounting and ERP vendors supply the pattern that matters most for interface design: a place to review what agents drafted. Microsoft's Business Central made a task pane for all agents generally available in its October 2026 release while its Copilot chat stayed in public preview ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-29-0)). NetSuite and Sage Intacct both announced "AI Workspaces" that put people, agents and approvals in one place ([Oracle release via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15814430728966144); [Sage](https://www.sage.com/en-us/news/press-releases/2026/11/sage-intacct-sets-out-its-vision-for-an-agentic-finance-platform-with-ai-workspaces/)), and Xero's JAX matches high-confidence transactions to bank feeds and explains each match, with the customer approving each step where it matches documents to transactions ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/media-releases/new-ai-innovations-xerocon-denver/)). In this category chat is no longer the headline. Named agents that do one job, and a queue where a person approves the result, are.

The second recurring element is the proactive push. Toast's scheduled agents check data and send a notification or email, for example about missed breaks or sold-out items ([Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Toast-IQ-Agents-Overview)); SpotOn's Profit AI delivers "proactive profit recommendations" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260602225359/en/SpotOn-Introduces-Profit-AI-Delivering-Proactive-Profit-Recommendations-to-Independent-Restaurants)); Fourth's iQ 3.0 adds "a new layer of above-store AI" that surfaces risks and recommended actions across an estate ([Fourth](https://www.fourth.com/press-release/fourth-launches-fourth-iq-3-0)). Frontline task platforms are built on the same idea without needing AI for it: the UK checklist product Trail, with over 245 million tasks completed by its vendor's count, is organised around the day's checklists and offers its assistant in a higher tier for questions on the job ([Access Group](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/hospitality/software/checklists/)).

### Generated interfaces ship as apps, reports and cards, never as transaction forms

The field has settled into three tiers of generated interface. In the controlled tier the product owns the components and the model only picks one and fills it; in the declarative tier the model emits a description that the client draws from a catalogue of approved components; in the open-ended tier the model returns markup or code that runs in a sandbox ([CopilotKit](https://copilotkit.ai/blog/the-developer-s-guide-to-generative-ui-in-2026)). Every vendor that puts generated interface in front of business users who change records stays in the first two tiers. Google's A2UI lets an agent request only components from a catalogue the client pre-approved and calls itself "a declarative data format, not executable code" ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/)); Microsoft's guidance is that a card should "gather everything they need in a single step" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/agents/design-guidelines/adaptive-cards-for-agent-design)); Salesforce renders structured components from model output across more than 4 million sessions and reports that its early system "frequently over-formatted responses" until the number of options per component was capped ([Salesforce Engineering](https://engineering.salesforce.com/how-agentforce-converts-llm-responses-into-structured-ui-for-ai-agents-across-4m-sessions/)). Free generation at run time remains slow and unreliable: Google says its generated pages "can sometimes take a minute or more" with "occasional inaccuracies" ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)), and reviewers of Anthropic's research preview found it "often failing to implement the functionality of a button" ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/anthropic-experiments-with-an-agent-for-gereating-ui-on-the-fly)).

Where commerce and ERP products do generate interface, they generate apps, reports and views that a person builds once and then keeps: Shopify merchants generate a custom admin app, test it and install it ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/winter-26-edition-merchant)), NetSuite's AI Portlets build a dashboard from a sentence ([Oracle release via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15814430728966144)), and ServiceNow generates multi-page experiences that a developer then owns ([ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/zUlSbxoxKPAWFDGUdAoSCw/Yh2z7SmzcIlQ4wMpeKqZdQ)). In restaurant software specifically, no vendor documentation of "take me to" navigation or of forms generated on the fly was found; the dedicated search did not run, so this is "not found", not "does not exist". Where restaurant vendors add AI to data entry they use voice for stock counts and photos for compliance checks ([Crunchtime](https://www.crunchtime.com/press/crunchtime-introduces-four-new-ai-capabilities-to-elevate-operations-management-lifecycle)), document reading for supplier paperwork ([Lightspeed via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/339502/lightspeed-commerce-launches-ai-powered-automation-to-help-retailers-eliminate-manual-inventory-entry)) and prompt-driven bulk edits of existing records ([SpotOn](https://www.spoton.com/solutions/ai-menu-assistant/)). Nobody is promising a generated-interface back office.

### Connectors are open, the assistant is free, and the UK is a cycle behind

Three side-currents shape the commercial picture. First, vendors now let operators bring their own assistant: Square's MCP connector lets an outside assistant "ask questions and make changes" through a defined set of actions ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/introducing-squaremates)), and the UK analytics vendor Tenzo answers questions inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot, pitched as "a shift away from dashboards and towards direct answers" ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/tenzo-launches-ai-connector-for-hospitality-operators)). Second, pricing follows an "assist free, act paid" pattern: Squaremates and Xero's JAX chat carry no extra charge ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/introducing-squaremates); [Xero](https://www.xero.com/au/ai-in-accounting/jax/)), Toast sells agents by job (Toast IQ Team is reported from USD 60 a month plus USD 9 per employee, a secondary source) ([Dynamic Business](https://dynamicbusiness.com/ai-tools/toast-iq-team-ai-labor-management-software.html)), Intuit is moving agents to consumption pricing ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/intuit-plots-ai-agent-expansion-august-consumption-model)), and Block says it is not ready to choose a definitive model ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/block-explores-how-to-price-ai/827204/)). Third, the acting agents are American: Squaremates is for US sellers, UK Square sellers received the earlier read-only Square AI in February 2026 ([Square UK](https://squareup.com/gb/en/press/square-ai)), and franchises are served last, since the Managerbot beta covered "most non-franchise" sellers ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/managerbot-open-beta)).

UK-rooted vendors have shipped narrower things: Fourth's above-store assistant, Access's Evo Copilot for natural-language questions over its till and checklist products ([Access Group](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/hospitality/evo-early-access/)), Tenzo's connector, Deliverect's menu agents and Flipdish's phone agent ([Flipdish](https://www.flipdish.com/gb/resources/blog/introducing-flipdish-ai-phone-agent-for-restaurants)). No operator-facing assistant was found for Zonal, Tevalis, Vita Mojo, Storekit or Loyverse, on one search each, which is weak evidence. Two gaps in vendor coverage matter for Macaroonie. Food safety is almost untouched by vendor AI apart from Crunchtime's photo checks, and cash reconciliation, tips and wages appeared in no vendor's AI announcement found. Capital, meanwhile, is going to operations layers above the till: Nory raised USD 37m in September 2025 and MarginEdge USD 80m in August 2026 ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/16/nory-closes-37m-series-b-to-automate-restaurant-operations-with-ai/); [Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/08/marginedge-raises-80-million-as-competition-for-the-ai-powered-restaurant-back-office-intensifies/)).

## Scrutiny: tried by half, used weekly by a quarter

### Reach is easy, habit is hard, and the outcome figures are vendor claims

Measured adoption depends on who asks. The US trade body finds about **39% of restaurant operators using AI in any form and 3.2% running it companywide**, with complexity the top concern ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/restaurants-are-still-taking-a-cautious-approach-to-ai); [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/hospitality/only-32-of-restaurant-operators-run-ai-companywide-nra-survey-finds)), while a till vendor's survey has 57% of UK managers actively using an AI tool ([Toast UK](https://pos.toasttab.com/uk/blog/on-the-line/ai-use-in-uk-restaurants-data-and-insights)). The spread is the finding: most "use" is someone in the business using a general chatbot or a feature a vendor switched on. A 2026 benchmark of 112 US restaurant leaders found that 64% had deployed no AI for operations and that the gap between top and bottom performers was explained by whether general managers use the tools they already have, not by AI adoption ([Fourth](https://www.fourth.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/State_of_Restaurant_Operations_2026.pdf)).

The best-resourced vendor shows the same shape in its own numbers. More than half of Toast locations had tried Toast IQ within four months of launch ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/toast-q4-2025-earnings-reveal-all-in-ai-strategy)), yet **40,000 locations were weekly active** in the first quarter of 2026 ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/toast-tost-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)) against a base that reached about 189,000 two quarters later ([Toast 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650164/000165016426000214/tost-20260930xexhibit991.htm)): roughly a fifth to a quarter, as an order of magnitude from figures in different quarters. Lightspeed reports about 20% of target restaurant locations adopting its restaurant AI ([Lightspeed call highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lightspeed-commerce-q4-earnings-call-151036920.html)). Direct AI revenue is small: Toast IQ Grow is on track for USD 10m of annual recurring revenue against a company run-rate of about USD 2.5bn ([TradingKey transcript](https://www.tradingkey.com/news/transcripts/262272635-)). Square's statement that its agent is now the main way sellers use the dashboard ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/square-makes-squaremates-ai-agent-the-main-dashboard-interface/)) rests on where the product is placed, not on published behaviour. Every outcome number found is a vendor pilot, and Gartner expects **over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027** for cost, unclear value or weak risk controls ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)).

### Chat lowers effort; it does not save time or improve accuracy

The controlled studies of 2025 and 2026 agree with two decades of design guidance. Among 134 industrial decision-makers a conversational interface lowered perceived workload against a dashboard, but its advantages "diminished as task complexity increased", neither interface was more accurate, and the conversational one "was not preferred as a sole basis for subsequent decisions" ([arXiv 2605.31287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31287)). In a 73-person study of an agent over a content system the benefit "may be lower interaction effort rather than faster task completion", and users "did not appear to avoid delegating riskier actions" ([arXiv 2608.19551](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19551)). In the one small comparison of voice against a screen for data maintenance the screen was faster, 179 seconds against 204 ([arXiv 2505.22303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22303)). Generated interfaces beat plain chat answers in over 70% of preference comparisons, most in structured, information-dense domains ([arXiv 2508.19227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19227)), which argues for cards and tables over paragraphs, not for chat over screens. Nielsen Norman Group names the underlying problem the articulation barrier and recommends conventional controls around the prompt ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prompt-controls-genai/)). The cautionary case is Microsoft 365 Copilot, where secondary reporting puts paid seats under 4.5% of commercial users and weekly use at 20% to 30% of those ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/microsoft-pushed-copilot-everywhere-but-barely-anyone-bought-it-and-even-fewer-use-it-report/)): a chat pane beside capable software does not create a habit. Two design consequences follow. Language earns its place for "find this" and "do this small thing"; pages win for "compare, check, decide". And because people hand over risky operations as readily as safe ones, the product, not the user, has to apply the extra friction.

### The frontline is new, noisy, shared and often working in a second language

Team turnover in UK hospitality ran at 67% in the year to autumn 2025 ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2025/09/30/internal-promotions-driving-lower-staff-turnover-in-hospitality-report-says)), so at any moment a large part of the user base is new and lightly trained; the interface is the training. Speech recognition carries a penalty for non-native speakers of about 11 points of error rate with one widely used model, narrowed to about 1.7 points by a newer model plus a correction pass ([GCN](https://gcn.com/?p=23126)), and kitchens are loud: one hospital kitchen averaged 80.87 dB(A) at peak ([JEST](https://jest.utm.my/index.php/jest/article/download/182/111)). Devices are shared: 95% of IT and security decision-makers had seen frontline staff stay signed in or share credentials in the past year ([Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/20/frontline-worker-authentication-passwordless)). The one behavioural dataset, a US vendor's assistant logs with no published method, shows **54% of prompts coming from phones and 53% arriving during peak lunch or dinner service**, against 15% at nightly close, with menu builds still done mostly on office computers ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/q1-2026-restaurant-ai-pos-trends)). That supports a split by device and moment, not one interface for everything.

On trust, UK managers say a clear explanation would most raise their confidence in an AI recommendation, and 65% report staff raising concerns about AI-made schedules ([Toast UK](https://pos.toasttab.com/uk/blog/on-the-line/why-ai-scheduling-succeeds-or-fails-on-how-you-explain-it)). The hardest evidence comes from retail replenishment: supermarket managers systematically changed automated order advice for reasons the system could not see ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.1090.1141)), and after a stock-out they pushed orders below the proposal and caused further stock-outs ([M&SOM](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/msom.2023.0426)). The lesson is editable proposals with a visible reason, not a yes or no on the whole thing. Alert fatigue is real (15% of UK IT respondents had deliberately ignored or suppressed alerts) ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366637587/Three-quarters-of-UK-IT-teams-beset-by-outages-due-to-missing-alerts)), and a study of 319 knowledge workers found higher confidence in AI went with less checking of its output ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/is-ai-ruining-our-skills-early-results-are-in-and-theyre-not-good/)). Three things are missing from the evidence: no independent time-use study of restaurant or shop managers, no controlled test of a task feed against dashboards, and no independent measurement of speech recognition in a working kitchen.

### Open-ended agents fail two tasks in three, and injection has no fix

On a realistic benchmark of sales and service tasks, leading agents succeed about 58% of the time in single-turn tasks and **about 35% in multi-turn settings**, while executing a defined workflow is their strongest skill at above 83% ([arXiv 2505.18878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18878)). Model-written SQL reaches only 39.1% on an enterprise-scale variant of a standard benchmark against 82.0% on the original ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1971/)), and supplying a curated semantic description of the data adds 17 to 23 percentage points for every model tested, on a small benchmark of 100 questions ([arXiv 2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)). The design that this supports is an assistant that maps a request onto a named, validated action or a named measure, and never plans freely or does the arithmetic itself.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre says prompt injection "may never be totally mitigated" because a model draws no inherent line between data and instruction ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection)). The 2025 ForcedLeak flaw began with text typed into a public web form that a staff agent later read ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/09/salesforce-patches-critical-forcedleak.html)), which is the shape of a booking note, a review or a supplier price list. Anthropic's own measurement has its best-defended model still successfully attacked 1.4% of the time under adaptive attack ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses)). The documented failures share one pattern: an assistant asserting something false with confidence (Air Canada's chatbot on fares, the Replit agent on whether deleted data could be recovered) while the business carried the loss ([McCarthy Tetrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/moffatt-v-air-canada-misrepresentation-ai-chatbot); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/22/replit_saastr_response/)). The firms that reversed, among them McDonald's on drive-through voice ordering and Klarna on customer service, kept the technology and restored a conventional route ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/mcdonalds-end-ai-drive-test-ibm-rcna157603); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-08/klarna-turns-from-ai-to-real-person-customer-service)). No documented failure of an assistant inside a restaurant back office was found, which reflects how new such products are more than how safe.

### The law leaves responsibility with the operator

None of this is legal advice, and the dates come from law-firm summaries, not primary texts. The EU moved its high-risk AI obligations, which include worker management, to 2 December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)), while its transparency duties are reported to have applied since 2 August 2026 ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)). In the UK the automated decision-making reforms took effect on 5 February 2026 ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html)), and the regulator's draft guidance says token human oversight is not enough: the person must have real authority to override and must understand the basis ([Stevens & Bolton](https://www.stevens-bolton.com/insights/102mpf8/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-draft-guidance-on-automated-decision-making-i/)). The competition regulator's March 2026 guidance holds a business responsible for its AI agent's conduct toward consumers even where a third party supplies it ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/aq-ai-agents-cma-publishes-guidance-on-complying-with-consumer-law)), mandatory written allergen information is proposed but not enacted ([C&C Solicitors](https://www.candcsolicitors.co.uk/blog/owens-law-spring-2026-fsa-allergen-review/)), and payroll records must be kept for three years ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/paye-for-employers/keeping-records)). For the interface this means four fixed lines: rota, pay and tip proposals stay drafts a manager edits; Pepper does not rank or score individual staff; allergen data read by a model needs a named person's sign-off; and the stored record and its audit trail stand independently of the conversation that produced them.

### At UK prices the model bill decides the margin

The UK's 100 largest restaurant groups made GBP 204m of profit on GBP 13.3bn of revenue in the year to mid-2026, about 1.5% ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/08/24/the-uks-top-100-restaurant-groups-see-profits-fall-by-nearly-half/)), and the adult minimum wage is GBP 12.71 an hour ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2025/11/26/national-living-wage-to-increase-by-41-to-1271/)). Self-serve tills list at GBP 69 a month per site at Square and GBP 199 to 265 for Zonal's rental bundles ([Square UK](https://squareup.com/gb/en/point-of-sale/restaurants/pricing); [Zonal](https://www.zonal.co.uk/products/rental-packages/3-in-1-tech-suite-for-independents)). Buyers at these margins ask for payback in months, and the researchers' own arithmetic puts a saved frontline hour at about GBP 16 to 17 fully loaded, so a GBP 100 a month product breaks even at about six saved hours a month. That is the unit Macaroonie's value claims should use.

On the cost side, AI products are reported to run at about 52% gross margin against 70% to 90% for conventional software ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin/)), and the top tenth of users account for close to 90% of usage ([Adamarant](https://adamarant.com/en/blog/ai-feature-token-cost-budget-it-before-it-eats-your-margin)). The table below is the researchers' estimate from published token prices ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md)) and stated assumptions (a three-call tool loop per request over a cached 20,000-token prefix), not measured data. Macaroonie already starts chats on the small model and moves to the main model for files and heavy tools, and its default allowance of 2,000,000 units a month per group at USD 4 per million units is about USD 8 of model spend ([api/src/ai/settings.js](../api/src/ai/settings.js)). By this report's arithmetic that allowance fits an assistant used as a helper and not one that carries most navigation and data entry on a larger model.

| Model tier (Nov 2026 prices) | Cost per request | Typical site, 600 requests a month | "AI-first" site, 3,000 a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (Haiku 5.5) | about USD 0.003 | USD 1.80 | USD 9 |
| Mid (Sonnet 5.5) | about USD 0.045 | USD 27 | USD 135 |
| Upper (Opus 5.5) | about USD 0.09 | USD 54 | USD 270 |

Against a bundled price of GBP 150 to 300 a site, 3,000 requests a month on the mid tier would take 35% to 70% of revenue. An AI-first product at UK prices therefore works only if page jumps cost no model call, routine work runs on the small model, and each venue has a fair-use ceiling.

## Macaroonie already owns the hard half

The repository analysis (static counts on 22 November 2026, nothing run against a server; internal links in this report are relative to the report's folder) shows a product that has built most of what the leaders' safety model requires and little of the surface that would make it a front door. The decisive design choices are already right. A change tool never writes to the database: it calls the page's own route as the signed-in person, so permissions, emails and pushes are the page's own, and a change is stored as a pending action and shown on a card built from the tool's plan, never from the model's text ([docs/claude/areas/pepper.md](../docs/claude/areas/pepper.md); [api/src/ai/chat.js](../api/src/ai/chat.js)). Reports are built from a catalogue that is "the only place table and column names come from", and "nobody, person or model, ever writes SQL" ([api/src/reports/catalogue](../api/src/reports/catalogue); [docs/REPORTS.md](../docs/REPORTS.md)). The Today feed is worked out from data and rules on every request, never by the model ([api/src/services/opsToday.js](../api/src/services/opsToday.js)). These match what Toast, Square and the security guidance arrive at independently.

Two findings set the shape of the work. Navigation by words is close: destinations, descriptions, search words and permissions are all data already, and the missing half is context in the address, because Cash Recon, Rota, Food safety, Timeline, Checklists, Staff and Schedule read nothing from their address and Cash Recon's day, week, wages and settings are four views held in state at one address ([docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md](../docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md); [admin/src/pages/CashRecon.jsx](../admin/src/pages/CashRecon.jsx)). Generated forms are far: the admin has 699 hand-built inputs, 7 client-side validation schemas against 560 in the API's routes, whole-tree saves that must send back every field, and panels that hold rules of their own such as a corrective action required when a reading is out of range ([admin/src](../admin/src); [api/src/routes](../api/src/routes); [admin/src/components/forms/FormSheet.jsx](../admin/src/components/forms/FormSheet.jsx)). The product's own requirements already say "the AI picks the form type; it never writes HTML or makes up fields" and "the person types the values" ([docs/CHAT_FIRST.md](../docs/CHAT_FIRST.md)), so freely generated forms would reverse a stated principle, not extend one.

Three further facts matter. Nothing about Pepper is proven in use: tool search, the report tools, streaming, model routing and the document readers are each recorded as tested only against a stand-in, and no evaluation set or usage record exists ([docs/claude/OUTSTANDING.md](../docs/claude/OUTSTANDING.md)). The Pepper-first shell is parked by the owner until design mock-ups exist, with its form cards, voice, notices and phone app built in the meantime ([docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md](../docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md)). And the four registries (modules, pages, report subjects, tools) share only a module key: nothing says in one place that a dish is the `menu_items` table, the `menu.dishes` subject, edited at `/menus?edit=` and changed by `update_dish`, and names differ between layers for staff, dishes, orders and venues ([api/src/reports/catalogue](../api/src/reports/catalogue)). A plan to move every hand-kept list into per-area records exists and is unbuilt ([docs/PARALLEL_WORK.md](../docs/PARALLEL_WORK.md)); it is the natural home for a framework.

## The interface to build: one home, one bar, filled forms, a review queue

### Three parts of the vision, three verdicts

| Part of the vision | Verdict | Build it as | Do not build it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural-language input and navigation | Yes, as the first place to ask | One bar that searches instantly without the model, hands over to Pepper on Enter, and answers with a link into a real page, a real form or a sourced figure | A full-screen chat that replaces the menu, or page jumps that depend on a model call |
| Dynamically generated forms | Not as stated | Real form panels that open sized to the task and pre-filled, plus plain set-up forms drawn the same way every time from the server's schema; the model chooses the form and proposes values | Fields, layout or validation composed by the model for each request |
| A platform-set data framework | Yes, and it is the most valuable part | One registry that joins each business object to its table, report subject, page, actions and form, with every action's consequence declared | A run-time metadata engine, tenant-defined tables, or model-written SQL |

The rule that ties the three together is the one Macaroonie's documents already state and the evidence supports: the model words, ranks, picks and routes; rules and data decide tasks, figures and what a card says; the person types measured values and confirms. Six working rules follow from the scrutiny. Navigation never needs the model. Every answer is a place or a form the person can find again without Pepper, so the assistant teaches the product instead of hiding it. The product sets friction by what an action does, since users will not. Everyone in a role sees the same frame, and the only thing that adapts is the person's own pins and a small, labelled strip of suggestions. Voice and photos are ways to fill a form, never ways to skip one. And every task stays possible with the model switched off, so a provider outage is a slower day and not a closed one.

### Home: the day comes to the person

Home replaces the three home pages (the Overview tiles, Today and the Launcher) with one page at `/`. From top to bottom it holds the ask bar; a strip of shortcut chips (the person's own pins, up to twelve, kept on the server, then at most four suggestions each carrying a plain reason such as "due now" or "you open this most Fridays"); the Today feed grouped as Late, Now, Next and Later, each task with one button that opens its form in a sheet; a week-ahead strip; and a "For review" tile with a count. The feed's content follows the person's permissions, which the feed already enforces by calling the pages' own routes as that person, and an area the person cannot see is named, not hidden. The Overview tiles stay as a page in the menu for people who compare figures at a desk; Launcher mode is retired and its tiles become pins.

The discipline that keeps Home useful is restraint: few items, each one something this person can act on, duplicates grouped, and "late" reserved for things that are. Every source earns its place by the share of its items acted on, and sources nobody acts on are pruned. This is a well-reasoned bet and not a proven result: it fits the observed habit of short questions on a phone mid-service and the stated wish of multi-unit operators for visibility of daily task completion (about 80% want it and 36% have it) ([Crunchtime](https://www.crunchtime.com/blog/webinar-recap-2025-restaurant-growth-insights-report)), and it matches Square's and Shopify's home screens, but nobody has published a test of a task feed against dashboards in this sector.

### The ask bar: search first, Pepper on Enter

There is one input, on Home and as an overlay on every other page (Ctrl or Cmd+K at a desk, a button on the rail on touch screens). As the person types, results appear from the local index with no model call: pages, saved reports and, once record search exists, records. A page result carries chips for the context that page takes (venue, day or week, tab) so that "rota" followed by two taps opens next week's rota at the right venue. Pressing Enter with nothing chosen, or tapping the "Ask Pepper" row that always sits last, hands the words to Pepper. This is the placement that has stuck elsewhere: Hashnode's command palette shows an AI suggestion that "takes you to the place you described", Sentri shows an "Ask AI" row only when a query matches no command well, and Stripe's assistant opens pages and performs small tasks where "you only need to confirm" ([Hashnode](https://hashnode.com/changelog/2026-07-28-ai-navigation-cmd-k); [Sentri](https://rameshbabuprudhvi.github.io/sentri/docs/guide/command-palette.html); [Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/assistant)).

Pepper answers in four card types and no others, as the existing plan already names them. A nav card is a link to a page with its context shown. A form card opens a real form. A data card shows a figure with what was counted (measure, venue, dates) and an "Open report" link that lands on the rows; the report engine computes it, and a question outside the catalogue gets "that is not something reports cover yet", not a guess. An action card is the Confirm card. Under the bar on tablets sits a row of suggested asks, because people at a host stand tap and do not type. Before anything opens or runs, the card names what was understood, including the matched venue, day and record, since names are where both typing and speech go wrong for a second-language, high-turnover workforce. The three page matchers that exist today (the local search, the model's page picker and the scorer inside `open_page` with its own synonym list) become one ([shared/navSearch.js](../shared/navSearch.js); [api/src/routes/navAi.js](../api/src/routes/navAi.js); [api/src/ai/tools/general.js](../api/src/ai/tools/general.js)).

### The filled form: Pepper proposes, the panel validates, the person saves

This is the answer to "dynamically generated forms". What is dynamic is which form appears, what it holds (this venue's own fridges at this check time, this week's staff) and which values are already in it. What is fixed is what a field is, how it validates and how it saves. A person says or types "bread delivery, 14 loaves short, van was 6 degrees", or photographs a delivery note; Pepper chooses the delivery check form and proposes values; the sheet opens with those values in place, each marked as filled by Pepper until the person touches or accepts it; the panel applies its own rules; one Save goes through the same route the page uses; and the conversation keeps a read-only summary line with an Edit button. Pepper fills only what the words or the document state. Measured values (a temperature, a cash count) are typed or spoken by the person and shown back before saving, never inferred. Every platform's form primitive presents all fields at once, and the studies point to language for getting many values in and the form for overview and correction, though no controlled study of model-filled forms against manual entry was found.

Two kinds of form sit behind the one pattern. Forms with lines, grids or rules of their own (a cash-up, a rota day, a week's wages, an order) stay as hand-built panels, and the catalogue grows from ten to the fourteen already specified and then to set-up tasks. Flat single-record forms (add a supplier, a staff member, an expense, a closure) can be drawn by one generic renderer from the server's validation schema, enriched with a label, help text and examples per field, so that one definition validates the request, defines Pepper's tool and draws the form; the conversion tooling exists ([Zod](https://github.com/colinhacks/zod/blob/main/packages/docs/content/json-schema.mdx)). The renderer is deterministic: same schema and role, same form. The boundary matches the one the Model Context Protocol draws for forms a tool may request, a flat object of primitive fields with no nesting ([MCP specification](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/blob/main/docs/specification/2025-11-25/client/elicitation.mdx)), and the experience of teams that pushed schema-driven forms further and found them breaking down once forms became state-dependent or large, on the account of a vendor that sells an alternative ([SurveyJS](https://dev.to/surveyjs/react-json-schema-forms-in-practice-why-they-break-down-and-how-surveyjs-fixes-the-architecture-17ik)).

### The Confirm card: show the change, set friction by consequence

The Confirm card becomes a decision gate, not a notification. It shows the change in the terms the person would check: from and to, the total, the name, the date, where it applies. Its fields are editable in place, so a wrong quantity is corrected on the card and not by starting again. Friction is set per action from a declared class, in four levels. Reads run and are logged. Reversible, low-cost changes (mark sold out, tick a check, re-send an email) show the card and, once undo exists, are candidates for "Done, with Undo" in groups that choose it. Consequential changes (prices, orders, a rota, a refund) always show the before and after and wait for a tap. Destructive or personal changes (delete, anonymise, anything to pay or tips, allergen data) need a second, different kind of check on screen, such as a typed word, and can never be confirmed by voice. Practitioner guidance in 2026 converges on this tiering and warns that approving everything "turns the system into another approval queue" ([Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/designing-for-ai-agents-8-ux-patterns-that-build-trust-in-2026-713a0c926933); [DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/human-in-the-loop-approval-enterprise-ai-agents)). With 97 change tools behind the same card today, rubber-stamping is the risk, and an injected instruction hidden in a plausible card is how it would be exploited.

### For review: where drafts wait and Pepper's changes are listed

For review is a new page with two tabs. "Waiting" holds everything a person has to look at before it takes effect: supplier orders awaiting approval, documents the readers have turned into lines (an invoice, a price list, a menu from a photo), and Confirm cards that were left unanswered, which today expire after 30 minutes and vanish. Each item shows the source beside the proposal (the photographed invoice next to the lines read from it, with changed prices marked), the reason for each suggested figure, editable lines, and Approve, Edit and Reject. Items of the same low-consequence kind can be approved together; anything touching money, pay or allergens cannot. "Done by Pepper" is the audit view of the existing action record: who asked, on which device, what was proposed, who confirmed, what changed, with Undo where an inverse exists and filters by venue and person for whoever runs several sites. This is the 2026 element Macaroonie lacks, and the one Microsoft made generally available ahead of its chat. It gives managers what they say raises their trust, an explanation they can read before they approve, and it gives a franchisor the record it will ask for the first time something goes wrong.

### The phone: short answers, hold to talk, capture

The phone app keeps its tiles and gains the same Home in one column, with the ask bar at the bottom within thumb reach. It is built for the jobs the telemetry shows people doing on a phone during service: a quick figure, who is on, a small staffing change, marking something sold out, logging a check. Menu builds and set-up stay on a large screen, and the cash-up at close is a guided, pre-filled form, not a conversation. Voice is hold to talk only, with the transcript on screen before anything happens, numbers shown back before they are saved, and the venue's own dish, supplier and staff names supplied to the step that corrects the transcript, since menu vocabulary, on a speech vendor's own account, is the main failure point in restaurant use ([Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/learn/ai-drive-thru-voice-ai-order-taking)). Typing and tapping are always present as equal routes. The camera is an input in its own right: a photographed invoice or delivery note goes to For review, and the barcode scanner feeds stock and receiving. On a shared tablet a person switch (a tap on a name and a short PIN, not a full sign-in) comes before any change, because without it the audit trail records a device and not a person.

### Above the store: which site is off, and why

For an owner or area manager, Home's feed is replaced by venues ranked by exceptions: late and missed tasks, cash-ups not submitted, orders waiting, with one tap into a venue's own feed and a cross-venue For review. Completion is shown by site, not by person. This is the franchise form of the proactive brief, it is where comparison tables earn their place, and it speaks to the finding that use of existing tools separates the best operators from the rest. It is also where Macaroonie can define what the large platforms have left open for franchises: which fields the group locks (brand standards, allergen data, the core menu) and which a venue controls (rota, ordering, sold out), with Pepper obeying the same lines as the screens and saying plainly when a lock is why it cannot do something.

### Under the surface: one registry that describes what exists

The data framework is a registry, not an engine. The platforms that generate tables, forms and interfaces from metadata were built metadata-first from their first day ([Frappe](https://github.com/frappe/frappe)); retrofitting that onto about 245 existing tables is a rewrite, while describing what exists is not. The registry gives each business object one record that names its table, its report subject, the page and address that opens one, the actions that change it and the form that edits it, using the report catalogue's subject names as the shared vocabulary. Each action declares, as data, its typed inputs, the permission it needs, its preconditions with a plain message for each, its consequence class and therefore its confirmation rule, an idempotency key, and its inverse where one exists. Palantir's action types are the clearest published model: an action can be submitted only if all its criteria pass, and each can be set "to run automatically or only after the user confirms" ([Palantir](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/action-types/submission-criteria/); [Palantir](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/agent-studio/tools)). Odoo's documentation states the failure mode the declaration prevents: a tool "will execute unconditionally, unless the code itself prevents it" ([Odoo](https://github.com/odoo/documentation/blob/19.0/content/applications/productivity/ai/server-actions.rst)). From that one record come the Confirm card, the audit entry, the tool description and the "why not" message when a rule blocks a request.

The catalogue deepens the same way. Synonyms, instructions and worked examples per field and measure, the idea an emerging semantic-layer standard calls `ai_context` ([Ossie specification](https://github.com/open-semantic-interchange/OSI/blob/main/core-spec/spec.md)), are the cheapest accuracy gain available, and tool examples raised accuracy on complex parameters from 72% to 90% in Anthropic's internal tests ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use)). Tenant custom fields, if added, are registered in the same field registry so reports, simple forms and Pepper pick them up without new code; tenant-defined tables are not on the path. Two standards deserve a place now and nothing more: a product code (GTIN) on inventory items and supplier products, and supplier invoices held as structured data, because the UK has confirmed mandatory e-invoicing for domestic business invoices from April 2029 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/10/uk-b2b-e-invoicing-roadmap-april-2029.html)).

Nothing in the evidence shows an opening in general retail for a hospitality-first newcomer; food-led and franchise retail that shares the same rota, food safety, purchasing and multi-site workflows is a hypothesis to test after the catalogue work, not a finding.

## Nine steps in order, each with a number that says it worked

### Measure first, because nothing is proven yet

The order below follows the dependencies in the code and the parked plan, with one change of emphasis: measurement comes before any new surface. Pepper's accuracy, speed and cost against the live model are unknown, and the page-use and bar-use records that would show whether a new home helps do not exist. Anthropic's guidance on agent evaluation fits a one-developer team: start with 20 to 50 simple tasks drawn from real failures, grade the end state of the environment and not what the agent says, and run each trial from a clean tenant ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). Targets in the table are suggestions anchored where a benchmark exists; before the first franchise is onboarded the usable measures are task success, reversal rate, time per task and cost per request on test tenants, and the adoption measures need live customers. New pages must load on demand, since the admin's main bundle sits at 5.3 MB of a 6 MiB limit ([docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md](../docs/PEPPER_FIRST.md)).

Two measures run through every step. The first is depth: the share of all changes in each area made through Pepper or a filled form and not through the page. It is the truest reading of "AI-first", it will differ widely between food safety checks and menu design, and its only public anchor is Shopify's report that Sidekick creates about two-thirds of one kind of automation ([AlphaSense](https://www.alpha-sense.com/earnings/shop/)). The second is hours saved per site per month for the commonest tasks, timed in the product, because that is the figure a buyer at a 1.5% margin will ask for.

Dynamically generated forms | Accept the reframing: a growing catalogue of real forms that arrive filled, and a deterministic renderer for flat set-up forms; the rule that the AI never makes up fields stays | Every vendor that lets AI change records constrains it to approved components; no controlled evidence for generated forms |

Whether "nothing changes without Confirm" stays universal | Keep it until undo exists and first customers produce reversal data; then let a group move a short named list of reversible, low-cost actions to "Done, with Undo"; never money, pay, prices, allergens, deletions or anything about a person | Approval fatigue and habituation; users delegate risky and safe actions alike; the voice-confirm switch for four check tools is the precedent |

## How far to trust this: direction solid, figures soft

The direction rests on convergence, not on any single source: what the leading products ship, what the controlled studies measure, what the firms that reversed went back to, and what security and data regulators require all point to the same hybrid. Individual numbers deserve much less weight, for four reasons. First, page fetches failed for almost every host the researchers tried (vendor sites, trade press, the research archive, the usability and regulator sites), so nearly every cited finding rests on a search engine's summary of the page and not on a reading of it; quotations and dates need checking at the link before they are repeated to a client or printed. The exception is the data-framework research, which read pages on Anthropic's, Microsoft's and the protocol project's sites and on GitHub in full. Second, each researcher's shared search budget ran out after 24 to about 43 searches, and planned searches never ran on: independent reviews and operator complaints about Toast IQ and Square's assistants; UK availability of the US vendors' AI features; natural-language navigation and generated forms in restaurant products; controlled studies of model-filled forms; command bars in other products; several frontline task platforms; voice in warehouses and on wearables; UK digital-skills and accessibility data; regulator guidance on AI and allergen information; other providers' model prices; analyst and benchmark reports; and the catalogue data models of the main retail platforms. Third, most adoption and outcome figures are produced by vendors about their own products, with definitions that differ between them. Fourth, nothing has been tested with Macaroonie's users, because the product is pre-production, and every internal count is static, from the files, on one date.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Should the back office be AI-first?" turns out to have a settled answer in the market, which is an agent on the first screen, approval before change and conventional screens underneath, and Macaroonie's existing principles already say the same. The open question is where a one-developer, pre-production product can be better than a vendor whose agent already reaches more than a million sellers ([PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/earnings/2026/block-puts-ai-to-work-taking-orders-and-preparing-purchases)), and the answer is not the model, which everyone rents, nor the chat. It is the registry: one schema across till, rota, purchasing, cash and food safety, with every action's consequence declared, so that the assistant, the Confirm card, the audit trail, the simple forms and the reports are all drawn from the same definition. That is what the owner's "smart ERP" instinct points at, and it is the part of the vision the evidence supports most strongly. It also lands on ground the incumbents have left empty: food safety, cash, tips and wages carry almost no vendor AI, and franchise structures are being served last.

Two implications follow for the next few months. The window is real but short, since the US leaders' agents are likely to leave beta and reach larger and franchise customers during 2027, and whether they arrive in the UK on that timetable is not known. And the first real franchise will be the only test that counts, so the instruments (the evaluation set, the usage record, the reversal rate, the cost per request) should exist before that customer does. "AI-first" should then be a number Macaroonie reports per area, the share of changes that went through Pepper and stayed unreversed, and not a claim it makes in advance.
