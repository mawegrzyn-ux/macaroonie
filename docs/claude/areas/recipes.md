Covers: recipes and prep recipes, the cost engine (`shared/costing.js`), item units and yield as costing reads them, the Recipes page, the recipes and costing modules, the links from menu dishes to recipes (the variation matrix), option rules, menu margins and cost targets, the recipe reader and drafts Pepper wrote, coverage by sales, menu engineering, cost scenarios and Push prices (phase 7a), price creep, cost alerts and `why_cost_changed` (phase 7b) (`docs/INVENTORY.md`).

Key files and mistakes to avoid for this area: read it before changing code here. What shipped and when is in `docs/claude/history/recipes.md`; the index of all areas is in `CLAUDE.md`. The plan is `docs/INVENTORY.md` (phases 1 to 3 and 7 are this area's; stock is phases 4 to 6).

## Mistakes to avoid

> Append-only: never delete an entry, only correct one that turns out to be wrong. New entries go at the end.

- **A cost is worked out by `shared/costing.js` from `loadCosting()`'s rows, never stored or worked out again** (27 Nov 2026, phase 1) — the Recipes page, the preview, the report subject Recipe costs and Pepper all call `recipeCost()` on the ctx `loadCosting()` reads. A new place that shows a cost (menu margins, a widget, usage from sales) loads the same ctx and calls the same functions; a cost column on a table would go stale the moment a supplier price changes.
- **Yield divides, never multiplies** (phase 1) — what has to be bought is the usable quantity / (yield / 100) (`asBought()`): 1.20 at 80% is 1.50, not 1.44. A line's own `yield_pct` beats the item's (`yieldOf()`); a prep recipe's cooking loss is on the recipe (`loss_pct`), never on its items.
- **A unit a line uses is free text, so a unit the item doesn't know is a gap, not a refusal** — the save keeps it (a reader in phase 3 will bring units nobody has defined yet) and `toBase()` names it ("Cheddar has no size for a tbsp"). What must refuse is the item's side: an own unit a recipe uses can't be deleted (409 `unit_in_use`), a renamed one is written into its recipe lines in the same transaction, and an item's base unit can't change while it has own units or recipe lines. A new way of changing an item's units must keep all three.
- **Costs need Costing View everywhere, and supplier prices inside the explanation also need purchasing prices** — `access()` in `routes/recipes.js` and `recipeView()` decide it for the routes, the catalogue gives every money field `needs: 'costing'` and the cost subject module `costing`, and Pepper reads only through the routes. Coverage and gaps carry no money and show with Recipes View. A new place showing a cost must take the same two levels; never compare `costing` with 'none' by hand (use `permissionAtLeast()`).
- **A recipe save is whole: PUT `/api/recipes/:id` requires every field (`RecipeSave` is strict) and replaces the lines** (rule 13) — the editor's three Saves each send `saveBody(server, parts)`: that card's draft over the stored recipe, so another card's unsaved changes are neither saved nor lost. A new caller (Pepper's change tools later, an import) must send every field it doesn't change too.
- **Nesting is checked across every recipe of the group on each save (`nestingProblem()`), not by the database** — the table only refuses a line naming its own recipe. Any new way of writing `recipe_lines` (an import, Pepper, copying between groups) must load every recipe and call it, or a loop or a chain deeper than five levels gets in; the engine then shows a gap instead of hanging, but the recipe can't be costed.
- **The editor's `SortableRows` items must be a stable array** — `SortableRows` (`components/staff/shared.jsx`) copies `items` into its own state in an effect on `[items]`; building the array inline (`value.map(...)` in the JSX) gives a new array each render, the effect sets state each render, and the page loops. Memoise it (`useMemo(..., [value])`), as `LinesCard` does.
- **A test that looks for money in a JSON answer must look for money keys, not digits** (27 Nov 2026) — `doesNotMatch(JSON.stringify(view), /8\.5|102/)` failed now and then because a random uuid held "102". Match the keys that carry money (`"cost":`, `"per_unit":`, `"price":`, `"explanation":`) instead.
- **What was paid counts only in the item's base unit as it is now** (27 Nov 2026, phase 1 review) — `purchase_order_lines.pack_base_qty` is in the unit the item had when it was booked in, and an item can change its base unit once its quotes are gone (received orders don't stop it). `loadReceipts()` keeps only lines with `pl.base_unit = i.base_unit`, and `itemCost()` drops a receipt whose `base_unit` differs: a tray of 24 each read as 24 g costed every line at £0.30 a gram with no gap. A new reader of what was paid (usage from sales, variance, food cost) filters the same way.
- **The engine's memo is for the ctx's own recipe object only** (phase 1 review) — `recipeCost()` remembers a result by id, venue and basis only when the recipe is the very object `ctx.recipes` holds; an edited copy with the same id was given the stored recipe's cost. Cost a changed recipe by putting it in its own ctx (as `/preview` does) or by passing the copy, never by changing a stored recipe's lines in place.
- **Every recipe save takes `lockRecipes()` before it loads the group's recipes** (phase 1 review) — the nesting check reads every recipe's lines without a lock, so two saves at once (A into B, B into A) each passed against the other's old lines and wrote a loop. A new writer of `recipe_lines` (an import, Pepper's change tools, a copy between groups) takes the same per-group advisory lock first.
- **A Pepper result names a recipe, never its id** (rule 19, phase 1 review) — the `recipe` tool's link is `/recipes?q=<name>` (the page starts its search with it), not `?recipe=<uuid>`. `api/test/recipes.test.mjs` fails on a uuid anywhere in what the two tools return.
- **Which recipe a dish uses is `recipeFor()` and only `recipeFor()`, scored as `taxFor()` scores a tax rule** (27 Nov 2026, phase 2) — `linkScore()` takes `RULE_WEIGHTS` from `shared/tax.js` (stream 4, order type 2, venue 1; a stream link's stored venue adds nothing, as a tax rule names a stream or venues, never both), so a takeaway at Soho's bar is costed with the same precedence its VAT is charged by. A new reader (usage from sales, a widget) calls it with the sale's `{ venue_id, revenue_stream_id, order_type_id }`; never pick a link by hand, and change the weights only in `shared/tax.js`.
- **Menu dishes and library options are pointed at by plain ids, and "on a menu" means the draft or what is live** (phase 2) — `recipe_links.dish_id`, `recipe_option_rules.dish_id` / `option_id` and `costing_settings.sections[].section_id` have no foreign keys (the menu save re-inserts dishes under the same ids). Checks and the sweep use `dishIdsOnMenus()`: a dish taken out of the draft but still live keeps its link until it is published away, and only then does the sweep mark it missing. A new table pointing at dishes does the same, and duplicating a menu must carry its rows to the copy's ids (`copyRecipeLinks()` is where).
- **A dish has one price on a menu; only its VAT changes with the venue, revenue stream and order type** (phase 2) — the margins take the price from `fromPrice()` (what the till and online charge) and the VAT from `taxFor()`. Don't add a per-stream price in a margins page or report: if price levels come, they come to `shared/orderPricing.js` and the margins read them from there.
- **A report field can't be called `where` (nor `from`, `sql`, `col`, `tenant`, `skip`)** (phase 2) — the catalogue test checks `catalogueView()`'s JSON holds none of those words, and a field key is in it. The links subject's scope field is `applies_to` for that reason.
- **An option with no rule finds its own lines by whole words, not `nameScore()` alone** (phase 2) — `nameScore('Onion', 'Red onion')` is 0.75, under `AUTO_MATCH`, so "No onion" found nothing. `namesOption()` takes every word of the option (a plural trimmed) being a word of the item's name, or the names matching. Prefer a rule (Takes out: Red onion) wherever the match is a guess; the step says "(found by its name)".
- **Pepper's tools pick a menu with `pickByName()`, never a substring filter** (phase 2) — `dish_cost` filtered dishes by `menu.includes('lunch')`, which also kept the copy "Lunch (copy)", and the dish then matched twice. `pickByName()` takes an exact name first.
- **A meal deal is sorted and banded at its highest cost %** (phase 2; corrected in the phase 2 review: it was the dearest picks' cost over the cheapest price) — its `cost_pct` is null; `cost_pct_min` / `cost_pct_max` carry the range and `band` is the highest's. A list, a report or Pepper that sorts by cost % reads `cost_pct_max` for a deal (the margins page's `sortRows()`, the work's `cost_pct`, `menu_margins`).
- **A meal deal's cost % pairs each pick's cost with what the guest pays for it** (27 Nov 2026, phase 2 review) — a choice's `price_pence` in a deal is an extra charge and an optional step adds to the bill, so the dearest picks' cost over the deal's cheapest price (`fromPrice()`) read 81.6% for a deal whose worst real figure was 51.4%. `dealRange()` takes each end over its own picks' price (the deal's price before extra charges, `fromPrice()` less `comboFromPrice(item, 0)`, plus those picks' charges); the cost % extremes are a ratio, found by Dinkelbach's method, never by taking the dearest picks. A new figure over a deal (a typical cost in phase 5, a scenario) pairs its picks with their price the same way.
